WHY IT MATTERS

What your photos say
behind your back

Every photo your phone takes carries a hidden label called metadata. You never see it, but anyone who saves the original file can read it in seconds.

One photo, five clues

Where you were
GPS coordinates, accurate to a few metres
When you were there
Date and time, to the second
Which phone you own
Model, and sometimes its serial number
Who you are
Your name, if an app added it
What you cropped out
A hidden thumbnail can keep the full original

“Don’t websites remove it anyway?”

Some big social networks strip most of it when you upload. Plenty of places hand over the original file untouched:

  • Email attachments
  • Photos sent as a “file” or “document” in chat apps
  • Cloud-storage share links
  • Forums, marketplaces and listing sites
  • Your own blog or portfolio

And even when a site does strip it, the site itself has already seen everything. Cleaning the photo first keeps it yours.

It has happened before

A fugitive, found by one photo

In December 2012, Vice published a photo of software founder John McAfee while he was on the run. The iPhone photo still had GPS coordinates in it, which placed him in Guatemala. He was arrested there days later.

Columbia Journalism Review
A military warning

In 2012 the US Army warned soldiers about geotagged photos. It described a 2007 attack in Iraq where photos posted online helped the enemy locate and destroy four helicopters.

U.S. Army, “Geotagging poses security risks”

What PhotoScrub can’t do

It removes the hidden data. It can’t hide what’s visible in the picture. Before you post, take a quick look for:

  • House numbers, street signs and car number plates
  • Your school or office logo, ID badges and uniforms
  • Reflections in windows, mirrors and sunglasses
  • Screens and documents in the background

Questions

Is my photo uploaded anywhere?

No. Everything happens inside your browser, on your device. The code is public, so anyone can check.

Will my photo lose quality?

No. PhotoScrub doesn’t re-save the image. It cuts the hidden data out of the file and leaves the picture exactly as it was.

Can I stop my phone adding location in the first place?

Yes. On most Android phones, open the Camera app’s settings and turn off location tags. On iPhone, go to Settings › Privacy & Security › Location Services › Camera and choose Never. Your phone still adds its model and the time, so scrubbing is still worth it.

Why keep the rotation info?

It only says which way is up. Without it, portrait photos would show up sideways. It reveals nothing about you.
